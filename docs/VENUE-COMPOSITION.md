# VENUE COMPOSITION — how a scene becomes ONE scene (W2b, 2026-08-13)

Commissioned by Joe's W2 verdict, verbatim in ROADMAP W2b: good set
pieces, no integration — "setting them next to each other in this way is
simplistic. Please consider the visual flow of the scene as a whole."
This is the composition counterpart to fae-research/grammar.md's palette
law: distilled rules, each with a CHECK, drawn from environment-art
composition practice (focal hierarchy, leading the eye, value-driven
direction), diorama/stagecraft doctrine (single-viewpoint layered
staging), and set-dressing craft (harmony, grounding) — sources at the
bottom — filtered through what this table actually is.

**The venue is a DIORAMA, not a level.** A level is walked; a venue is
WATCHED, from one composed eye (the resting eye — the frame a player
lives in) plus one secondary (the spread, during rolls). Every rule
below is evaluated AT THE RESTING EYE first. This is a stronger
constraint than games get, and it is why the rules can be mechanical.

## The rules

1. **One hero; everything else subordinate.** The venue has exactly one
   architectural focal point (the glade's is the tower), and when dice
   are down THEY outrank it (grammar rule 2 — the dice are Ori). A
   supporting feature that demands attention is a defect wherever it
   stands. CHECK: rank the resting frame's contrast events; only
   dice > hero > supports order is legal.
2. **The eye gets a CIRCUIT, not a list.** Design the path: where the
   eye enters (the hero), where it falls (the hero's line to the
   resolve area), where it sweeps (the supports), where it releases
   (sky/mist). Features ON the circuit connect; anything off it is an
   island — which is exactly the three-set-pieces failure. CHECK: trace
   the path on a frame with a finger; every hop must ride a line, a
   value gradient, or an overlap — never a leap across dead ground.
3. **Overlap beats adjacency.** From the eye, feature silhouettes should
   overlap each other or a shared band (the mist, a ground gradient) —
   three separated shapes with gaps of flat ground read as furniture in
   a showroom. CHECK: count silhouette separations along the ground
   band; adjacent features share an edge, an overlap, or tissue (rule 4).
4. **The space BETWEEN features is designed.** Spill, trails, gradients,
   reach: each feature bleeds toward its neighbors, and no feature's
   boundary ends in a hard rim on featureless ground (the pasted-prop
   rim). Transition zones are real content: a moot spills strays, roots
   disturb their soil, water dampens its banks. CHECK: sample every
   between-region; every feature base must fade, spill, or reach.
5. **Three depth layers, all occupied, all overlapping.** Foreground
   (the resolve floor and its furniture), midground (hero + supports),
   background (mist band, treeline, sky). Haze unifies; each layer cuts
   behind the one in front. A support pushed INTO the background band
   couples the layers. CHECK: assign every feature a layer; no empty
   layer; the band visibly passes BEHIND every midground silhouette.
6. **Triangles, not rows.** Supports and hero form a depth-asymmetric
   triangle in plan — unequal distances, unequal depths, never two
   features at the same range reading as bookends — with the resolve
   area held inside it. CHECK: draw the plan triangle; no
   near-collinearity; no two features within ~10% of the same depth.
7. **Every directional element agrees.** Leading lines are the circuit's
   rails: ramps, beams, gap-facings, glints, headings. Each points INTO
   the circuit; none points off-stage. CHECK: list every directional
   element and what it points at; a single dissenter is the finding.
8. **Value carries; color decorates.** The circuit must survive
   grayscale — direction lives in the placement of light against dark
   (the strongest tool the craft names), not in hue. CHECK: desaturate
   the resting frame; walk rule 2's trace again.
9. **Nothing floats.** Every feature sits IN the ground: contact
   darkening, a skirt, disturbed soil, a bank. CHECK: every base, at the
   eye's distance, transitions rather than terminates.
10. **Composition works INSIDE the placement law.** The stage serves the
    table: dice-unreachable flanks (beyond the widest back wall, outside
    the tower envelope), the beam on the resolve area, sheets below die
    tops, results readable. venue-set asserts it; composition never
    argues with it. CHECK: the venue-set scenario stays green.

## The glade, diagnosed (what W2b changes)

Against the rules, the W2 room's specific findings:

- **R6 broken**: moot (−6.8, −6.6) and pool (+6.6, −6.4) sit at the SAME
  depth on mirrored flanks — bookends, not a triangle.
- **R3/R4 broken**: both supports end in hard rims on featureless
  ground; the ground between socket↔moot and socket↔pool is dead.
- **R2 broken at one hop**: the eye leaves the moot with nowhere to go —
  no tissue back to the tower's roots.
- **R7 mostly passing** (wisp heads for the moot gap, the gap faces the
  clearing, beam lands on the resolve area) — the glint is the
  dissenter: its axis copies the beam tilt instead of pointing anywhere.
- **R5 half-passing**: the pool reads midground; nothing couples
  midground to the background band.

The fixes (composition only — living things are W5's): the pool moves
BACK into the mist's edge and slightly out (+7.2, −7.4), its glint
re-aimed at the tower's foot; a spill of strays walks from the moot's
tower-side edge toward the root flare; the ground bakes two gradient
lobes of moss/detail connecting the socket to each flank; both features
gain base transitions (damp bank ring, trampled ring); the mist band's
bites bias thinner over the moot (the circuit's release point) and
denser behind the pool.
