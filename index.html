Help

Fair rolls, one table

Online, the server does the rolling. Values come from cryptographically strong randomness on the server; your notation is re-read there, and nothing a device sends can pick or nudge a result — the roller has no more influence over the dice than anyone watching.

Every screen watches the same throw. A roll travels as its landed values plus a small pose seed; each device replays the identical tumble from that seed, and every die is corrected to land exactly on the server's value. The motion is presentation — synchronized theater. The values are the record. The pose seed is drawn independently of the values, so it reveals nothing.

Hidden rolls are absent, not covered: values a seat is not meant to see never reach that seat's device at all.

Solo tables run entirely in your browser with local randomness — the same dice, no wire.

Systems are a lens

A roll stores raw facts: which dice, which faces, what was asked of them. The room's system decides how those facts read — Your Soul Deal reads each die against its own rank column and never sums; the totals systems read the summed value, with targets and modifiers. Switching the system re-reads the whole log in place: history does not change, its meaning does.

Roll notation

3d6+2 — three d6 plus a flat bonus. adv / dis — pair every d20, keep the better / worse. kh2 kl2 dh1 dl1 — keep or drop by landed value. ro<=2 — reroll low faces, once per die. ! — max faces explode, up to three deep. dc 15 — a target to beat. # words — label the roll. check / cinematic — stage the moment.

Visibility

Open — everyone sees the roll and the result. Face down — the table sees dice land; the values stay yours until you reveal. Only me — nobody else learns the values. Whisper to… — you pick the audience; the rest of the table sees that a roll happened, not what it said.

Pool stats — how dice combine

Under Your Soul Deal, dice never sum. Each die reads its own face against its own rank column, so a pool forecasts as per-rank spectra: every segment is that die's exact chance of landing that outcome — matching faces ÷ total faces.

The one-line view is the count-weighted average of those spectra. For an outcome O in a pool of N dice, where the pool holds n₁ dice with chance p₁(O), n₂ with p₂(O), and so on:

P(O) = (n₁·p₁(O) + n₂·p₂(O) + …) ÷ N

— the chance that one die drawn at random from this pool lands O. It is a view over the same per-die math, not a new read: the expanded view shows the exact rows, and landed results are never merged.

Keep/drop (kh kl dh dl) shows no per-die forecast: which dice count is decided by the landing itself.

Under the totals systems the line is the exact min / average / max of the summed roll. In rare pools where the 40-die cap can cut rerolls or explosions short, exact math is impossible before the landing — the line is then estimated from 4,000 seeded sample rolls, and says so.

Your pools

Saved pools are named dice on shelves — Attributes, Skills, Motivations, or shelves you make. A pool stores its full spelling — dice, modifiers, target, privacy — and that whole intent travels together wherever the pool is rolled or shared.

Dice value is a build-time measure of a pool or shelf: the sum of every physical die's highest face. Advantage partners count (they always hit the felt); reroll replacements and explosion children do not (they only exist after a value lands); modifiers are ignored. Two spellings of the same physical dice always price the same.

Your data

Your pools live in your browser, per site — there are no accounts. A room lives in server memory while the table stands and evaporates afterwards; its log is the room's record for as long as it lasts. Pools travel by export and import: a small portable text file you hand around yourself. Links carry a room name and nothing else about you.
